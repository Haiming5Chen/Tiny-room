# Contact-pair friction

Generated by `scripts/contact_pairs_doc.py` from `src/little_world/materials/contact_pairs.py`.
Do not edit by hand; `tests/test_contact_pairs.py` fails when the two disagree.

Friction is a property of a pair of surfaces. For every contact between two surfaces
listed below, the MuJoCo adapter replaces MuJoCo's own combination (the larger of the
two geoms' coefficients) with the pair's **kinetic** coefficient, after collision
detection and before the solver (ADR 0010). Contacts between surfaces with no row
keep MuJoCo's combination and count as unsourced.

## Surfaces

- `abs`: ABS or a similar rigid thermoplastic
- `aluminium`: Aluminium alloy, bare or anodized
- `concrete`: Smooth dry concrete
- `hdpe`: High-density polyethylene
- `ptfe`: PTFE glide or skid
- `rubber`: Tread, bumper or pad rubber of the NR/SBR class, roughly 60-70 Shore A
- `steel`: Carbon or mild steel, as received
- `wood`: Dry planed, varnished or painted wood

## Conditions and spread

- **abs / abs** (0.74): ABS pin on ABS disc, 0.74-0.98 depending on load and speed; the lower bound is used. Self-mated ABS is unusually sticky; printed PLA on PLA measures 0.12-0.38.
- **abs / rubber** (0.50): No rubber-on-ABS value found. Uses the guideline for a rubber mat on polyamide (PA), the nearest rigid thermoplastic listed.
- **abs / steel** (0.37): ABS pin on steel disc 0.37-0.45 (Kumar & Roy); ABS against carbon steel, ball on flat, static 0.40 and kinetic 0.27 (ASM).
- **aluminium / aluminium** (0.34): 6061-T6 on 6061-T6, flat on flat. Tables from 1929-1940 give 1.05-1.4 for rigorously clean aluminium; as-received surfaces measure far lower.
- **aluminium / hdpe** (0.11): Plastic pallet on grooved aluminium, friction factor 0.15. The plastic is not named; pallets of this kind are usually HDPE or PP.
- **aluminium / ptfe** (0.10): No measurement found. PTFE's friction is set largely by its own low shear strength once a transfer film forms, so the polished-steel value is used.
- **aluminium / rubber** (0.40): Rubber friction mat on aluminium, sliding. Silicone pads on aluminium measure 0.4-0.6 in soft-robotics work; elastomer friction falls as pressure rises.
- **aluminium / steel** (0.32): 6061-T6 against 1032 steel, flat on flat: 0.35/0.25 and 0.47/0.38 depending on which specimen moves; the mean is used.
- **aluminium / wood** (0.19): Planed wood on grooved aluminium, friction factor 0.25 (static 0.27, dynamic 0.19). Consistent with modern wood-on-smooth-steel measurements (0.17-0.26).
- **concrete / concrete** (0.40): Concrete on concrete, sliding. Measured static values run 0.5-0.9 for as-cast surfaces.
- **concrete / ptfe** (0.10): No measurement found; polished-steel value used as an analogue. Likely an underestimate on rough concrete.
- **concrete / rubber** (0.95): SBR on smooth concrete, 0.16-0.61 MPa, 3 mm/s, 20 C: mean 0.95; filled SBR and NR on rough concrete 1.02-1.07. Textbook tables give 0.6-0.85.
- **concrete / steel** (0.57): Rolled steel plate on cast concrete, dry, 60 psi: peak 0.69, effective (after the peak) 0.57. The effective value is used as the sliding coefficient.
- **concrete / wood** (0.40): Concrete/wood sliding 0.4 (GDV); sawn timber on smooth concrete friction factor 0.55, static 0.59 (CTU Code).
- **hdpe / rubber** (0.70): Rubber friction mat (gum) on polyethylene, sliding.
- **hdpe / steel** (0.23): HDPE against carbon steel, ball on flat.
- **hdpe / wood** (0.15): Plastic pallet on plywood, friction factor 0.2. The plastic is not named.
- **ptfe / rubber** (0.30): Average of five rubbers sled-tested on a PTFE counterface.
- **ptfe / steel** (0.10): Static 0.05-0.08 at 500 psi; dynamic 0.10 at 10 fpm, 0.13 at 100 fpm. PTFE's static coefficient is lower than its dynamic one at low speed.
- **ptfe / wood** (0.10): No measurement found; polished-steel value used as an analogue. Likely an underestimate on rough wood, where no transfer film forms at first.
- **rubber / rubber** (1.10): Clean dry rubber on rubber: 1.2 at 35 kPa, 1.1 at about 85 kPa, 0.3 at 700 kPa. Strongly pressure dependent.
- **rubber / steel** (0.52): Average of five rubbers sled-tested on smooth ground steel. Rubber supplier tables give 0.6-0.9.
- **rubber / wood** (0.75): Rubber friction mat on matt varnished wood, sliding. A cargo-securing guideline, not a laboratory measurement of tread rubber.
- **steel / steel** (0.23): 1032 steel on 1032 steel, flat on flat. Rigorously cleaned steel measures 0.7-0.8; as-received surfaces 0.2-0.3.
- **steel / wood** (0.17): Chestnut sawn timber on S235 plate (Ra 0.33 um), 0.5 MPa, 8 mm/min. Rough steel measures up to 0.8.
- **wood / wood** (0.36): Sawn chestnut on sawn chestnut; grain orientation made no significant difference. Planed faces measure lower (0.1-0.3), Morin's oak 0.48.

## Limits

- MuJoCo has one sliding coefficient per contact; static values are recorded but
  not simulated, so there is no stick-slip.
- Elastomer friction falls as contact pressure rises; MuJoCo point contacts carry no
  contact area, so rubber values are mid-range, not pressure-resolved.
- Dry, clean, room temperature. Water, dust and temperature are not modelled yet.
- Sources for the same nominal pair routinely differ by a factor of two; the notes
  above give the spread behind each chosen value.
